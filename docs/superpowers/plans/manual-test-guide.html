Loanstar · AR & LRA Money-Movement Fixes

Checking the Offset, Other Loan & PDC fixes

Six problems were found and fixed across how "Offset"/"Other Loan" deductions move money between accounts, and how the PDC check schedule gets its start date during release. This is a plain-language walkthrough so you can see each fix for yourself — no technical background needed.

What's in this checklist

  • 1 A confirm that hiccups shouldn't be able to double-charge an account already verified
  • 2 Two confirms happening at once shouldn't lose one of them already verified
  • 3 Confirming a deduction that would do nothing now says so you can test this
  • 4 Past transfers now have a history you can look up you can test this
  • 5 The same loan account can no longer be picked twice you can test this
  • 6 PDC's first check date now locks to the computed date you can test this
Test 1 of 5

A confirm that hiccups shouldn't double-charge

Already verified

If AR's connection dropped at the exact wrong moment while confirming an Offset or Other Loan deduction, it used to be possible for that deduction to accidentally get applied twice — taking too much money off the target loan. We changed how a confirm is saved so it either fully happens once, or doesn't happen at all. There's no longer a way for it to land twice.

Why you can't click-test this one

This only shows up during an actual connection hiccup at the exact wrong instant — not something that can be triggered from the screen. I tested it directly against the system's records instead: confirmed a transfer, then deliberately tried to confirm it a second time to imitate a hiccup-and-retry.

What I found: the second attempt was refused outright, and the account's balance, the loan's payment record, and the transfer's own status all showed exactly one confirm had happened — never two.

Optional sanity check for you

  • Confirm any normal, healthy pending Offset or Other Loan transfer in AR > Internal transfers as you normally would.
✓
What you should see

It posts normally — the target account's balance goes down by exactly the transfer amount, once. This confirms the fix didn't change anything about how a normal confirm behaves.

Test 2 of 5

Two confirms at once shouldn't lose one

Already verified

If two different Offset/Other Loan transfers pointed at the same loan account and got confirmed within the same split second — say, two AR staff working at once — one of the two balance reductions used to be able to silently vanish, leaving the account under-reduced with no error. We changed how the system saves a balance update so two confirms in quick succession line up one after another instead of colliding.

Why you can't click-test this one

It needs two confirms landing at almost the exact same millisecond, which isn't realistic to reproduce by hand. I tested it directly instead: fired two different transfers at the same target account at the same time.

What I found: both reductions came through correctly — nothing was lost, and the account's final balance reflected both transfers, not just one.

Test 3 of 5

Confirming a "does-nothing" deduction now says so

You can test this

If the target loan of a pending deduction got fully paid off through some other route before AR got around to confirming it — a real payment, a write-off, another transfer — confirming it used to say "Posted" as if it worked, while actually doing nothing at all. Now it refuses and tells AR plainly that the account is already settled, so they know to reject it instead.

Steps (use a test loan, not a real client's)

  1. Set up a loan with an Offset or Other Loan deduction pointing at a second, existing loan account. Release it so the deduction lands as pending in AR > Internal transfers.
  2. Before confirming that pending transfer, fully pay off the target loan account through a normal payment (or a different transfer) so its balance reaches zero.
  3. Now go back to AR > Internal transfers and click Confirm on the original pending deduction.
✓
What you should see

Instead of "Posted," you'll see an error explaining the target account already has no balance left and that this transfer should be rejected instead. The account's balance won't move, and the transfer stays in the pending list so it can be rejected with a reason.

Test 4 of 5

Past transfers now have a history

You can test this

Once AR confirmed or rejected an Offset/Other Loan transfer, it used to disappear with nowhere to look at it again — a rejected one especially, since the reason it was rejected wasn't shown anywhere. There's now a proper history tab for it.

Steps

  1. Go to AR > Posting History.
  2. Click the new Internal transfers tab at the top.
✓
What you should see

A list of every past confirmed and rejected Offset/Other Loan transfer, with the account, the amount, whether it was Posted or Rejected, who resolved it, when, and — for a rejected one — the reason given. You can also search, filter, and sort it, the same way the other history tabs on that page work.

Test 5 of 6

The same account can't be picked twice

You can test this

The computation screen already stopped CSA/Committee from picking the same loan account twice within Other Loan rows, or twice within Offset entries — that part isn't new. What changed is that the same rule is now also enforced behind the scenes, so it can't be gotten around by anything other than the normal screen.

Steps

  1. Open a computation with Other Loan deductions (CSA new application, or Committee's override).
  2. Add an Other Loan row and pick a target account.
  3. Add a second Other Loan row and open its account dropdown.
✓
What you should see

The account you already picked in the first row is no longer offered in the second row's list — there's nothing to click that would create a duplicate, which is the visible proof both the screen and the system now agree on this rule.

Test 6 of 6

PDC's first check date now locks to the computed date

You can test this

This one's from a separate but related check: when a loan is released and the physical post-dated checks (PDC) get set up, the very first check's date used to be a blank box someone had to type into by hand — completely disconnected from the payment start date the system had already calculated. Now it works the same way the box right next to it ("Monthly amount") always has: filled in automatically, and locked so it can't be changed.

Steps

  1. Open a released (or ready-to-release) application's LRA workspace, on the "With PDC" path.
  2. Scroll to the PDC section where checks are set up.
  3. Look at the First check date box, right next to Monthly amount.
✓
What you should see

The box already has a date in it — greyed out, same as "Monthly amount" next to it — and clicking or typing into it does nothing. If you check that application's BLRI document, the "PAYMENT STARTS" date printed there should match exactly.

The part you can't accidentally break anymore

Since the box is locked, there's no click-path left to test "what if the date is wrong" — that's the point, it can't happen from the screen anymore. I tested the hidden safety net directly instead: built a real loan record, then tried submitting a PDC schedule with dates that didn't match the computed one.

What I found

It was rejected outright, with a message naming exactly which date it expected. The same submission with the correct dates saved normally. So even a request that skipped the screen entirely would still be caught.

Loanstar · CSA & Committee Computation

Checking the SME & Individual calculator changes

Business (SME) and individual loans used to be computed against one fixed generic rate — the calculator had no way to enter what a specific borrower actually negotiated. Now Interest, Processing Fee, Admin Fee, and Chattel Mortgage Fee (CMF) are typed in directly, Committee remembers what was already entered instead of quietly resetting it, and a returning borrower's last rate shows up as a starting point. Seafarer's calculator is untouched throughout.

What's in this part

  • 1 SME's rate is now typed in, not fixed you can test this
  • 2 Individual loans get the same fields, with Admin/CMF optional you can test this
  • 3 Committee's override no longer resets rates it didn't touch you can test this
  • 4 A returning borrower's last rate shows up as a starting point you can test this
  • 5 SME's first payment date now counts differently than Seafarer's you can test this
SME Test 1 of 5

SME's rate is now typed in, not fixed

You can test this

Before, every business (SME) loan used the same generic rate — 3% interest, 8% processing fee — no matter which account it was actually for, because there was only ever one SME product to pick from. Now the computation screen has four boxes you type directly into: Interest, Processing fee, Admin fee, and Chattel mortgage fee (CMF) — so whatever was actually negotiated for that borrower is what gets used.

Steps

  1. Open a business/SME application in CSA and go to its computation screen.
  2. Type in a rate that's clearly different from the old generic one — e.g. Interest: 2.5, Processing fee: 7, Admin fee: 1, CMF: 0.
  3. Fill in the amount and terms as normal, then compute.
✓
What you should see

The computed numbers reflect the rates you typed — not 3%/8%. The Loan type dropdown above it still exists (it just labels the loan for documents now), but changing your typed rate is what actually changes the math.

SME Test 2 of 5

Individual loans get the same fields — Admin/CMF optional

You can test this

Individual loans (the ones where a person applies alone, not a company) use the same calculator as SME. Most individual loans don't carry an admin fee or a chattel mortgage fee, so those two boxes start at zero and don't need to be filled in — but they're still there and still editable for the rare case that needs them.

Steps

  1. Open an individual application in CSA and go to its computation screen.
  2. Look at the four rate boxes — Interest, Processing fee, Admin fee, CMF.
  3. Fill in only Interest and Processing fee, leave Admin fee and CMF at 0, and compute.
✓
What you should see

It computes normally without asking you to fill in Admin fee or CMF — no red required-field warning on those two. The computation still goes through using just Interest and Processing fee.

SME Test 3 of 5

Committee's override no longer resets rates it didn't touch

You can test this

Before, if Committee only wanted to adjust the loan amount and left the rate boxes alone, the rates CSA had typed in used to silently get wiped back to the old generic 3%/8% behind the scenes — even though nothing on screen looked wrong. Now, leaving a rate box untouched keeps whatever was already there.

Steps

  1. Using the SME application from Test 1 (with your custom rates already on it), open it in Committee.
  2. Change only the Amount field — leave Interest/Processing fee/Admin fee/CMF exactly as they are.
  3. Submit the override.
✓
What you should see

The rates stay exactly what CSA typed (2.5%/7%/1%/0% from Test 1) — only the amount changed. Then try it the other way: change one of the rate boxes too, submit again, and confirm that new rate is what actually takes effect this time.

SME Test 4 of 5

A returning borrower's last rate shows up as a starting point

You can test this

When a borrower who's had an SME or individual loan before applies again, the four rate boxes now pre-fill with whatever rate was used on their last loan — just as a convenient starting point, not a locked value. It's shown alongside as a small reference list too, so you can see their history at a glance.

Steps

  1. Pick a borrower who already has at least one prior SME (or individual) loan computed in the system — or use the borrower from Test 1.
  2. Start a brand-new application for that same borrower, same segment, and open its computation screen before computing anything.
✓
What you should see

A small Previous rates for this borrower box listing their past loan(s) and what rate each used. The four rate boxes above it are already filled in with the most recent one — but you can still change them freely before computing.

SME Test 5 of 5

SME's first payment date now counts differently than Seafarer's

You can test this

Seafarer loans count the first payment using a cutoff rule tied to the 22nd of the month. Business (SME) loans don't work that way — their first payment is simply one month after release, on the same day of the month. SME computations used to borrow Seafarer's rule by mistake; now they use their own.

Steps

  1. Compute an SME loan with a known release date — e.g. release date August 24.
  2. Look at the First payment date line in the results panel.
✓
What you should see

For an August 24 release, the first payment date reads September 24 — exactly one month later, same day. Do the same for a Seafarer or individual loan with the same release date, and its first payment date should be unchanged from before (still following the 22nd-cutoff rule).

Loanstar · Individual Loans (MPL & Salary)

Checking the MPL & Salary payment schedule

Individual loans used to have no way to tell an MPL loan apart from a Salary loan — both were computed the same generic way. Now you pick which one it is when the application is created, and each gets the payment schedule it's actually supposed to have: MPL pays once a month like SME; Salary pays twice a month, on the 15th and the last day. This reaches all the way through to the PDC checks and the real collection schedule, not just the computation screen.

What's in this part

  • 1 Individual applications now ask MPL or Salary you can test this
  • 2 MPL's first payment date — same rule as SME you can test this
  • 3 Salary's first payment lands on the 15th or the end of the month you can test this
  • 4 Salary's PDC schedule has 12 checks, not 6 you can test this
  • 5 The real collection schedule matches the PDC checks exactly already verified
MPL/Salary Test 1 of 5

Individual applications now ask MPL or Salary

You can test this

Before, starting an individual application (no collateral) didn't ask anything about what kind of individual loan it was — MPL and Salary borrowers were treated identically. Now there's a required choice right after picking "Clean (no collateral)."

Steps

  1. Go to CSA > Create application.
  2. Set Loan segment to Individual.
  3. Set Collateral to Clean (no collateral).
✓
What you should see

A new required box appears — Individual loan type — with two choices: MPL and Salary. Try submitting without picking one; it should refuse to save until you do. Picking "Car Refinancing" or "Real Estate" instead makes this box disappear again — it's only for clean/no-collateral loans.

MPL/Salary Test 2 of 5

MPL's first payment date — same rule as SME

You can test this

MPL pays once a month, starting exactly one month after release, on the same day of the month — identical to how business (SME) loans already work.

Steps

  1. Create (or open) an individual application with Individual loan type: MPL.
  2. Compute it with a release date of August 24.
  3. Look at the First payment date line in the results panel.
✓
What you should see

The first payment date reads September 24 — exactly one month later, same day. No twice-a-month split for MPL.

MPL/Salary Test 3 of 5

Salary's first payment lands on the 15th or the end of the month

You can test this

Salary loans pay twice a month — the 15th and the last day ("kinsenas-katapusan"). The very first payment is whichever of those two dates comes next after release.

Steps

  1. Create (or open) an individual application with Individual loan type: Salary.
  2. Compute it with a release date of August 24 (after the 15th).
  3. Look at the First payment date line, then try again with a release date of August 10 (before the 15th).
✓
What you should see

Released Aug 24 (past the 15th) → first payment August 31 (end of month, the next date available). Released Aug 10 (before the 15th) → first payment August 15 instead.

MPL/Salary Test 4 of 5

Salary's PDC schedule has 12 checks, not 6

You can test this

Since Salary pays twice a month, a 6-term loan needs 12 actual post-dated checks, not 6 — each for half the usual monthly amount, alternating between the 15th and the end of the month.

Steps

  1. Take the Salary application from Test 3 through to LRA's PDC encoding step (with 6 terms).
  2. Click Build schedule.
✓
What you should see

12 rows, not 6 — dates alternating between a 15th and an end-of-month, each about two weeks apart. Each amount is roughly half the "Monthly amount" shown above, except the very last one, which may be a centavo or two different — that's the schedule correcting for rounding so the 12 checks add up to exactly the right total.

The part that's locked behind the scenes

Just like SME's PDC lock, you can't submit a Salary schedule with the wrong count, wrong dates, or wrong amounts — the system checks it and refuses, naming exactly what it expected. I tested this directly: a schedule with 10 checks instead of 12, and one with a check dated years off, were both rejected outright with the correct expected value named in the error.

MPL/Salary Test 5 of 5

The real collection schedule matches the PDC checks exactly

Already verified

Beyond the PDC screen, there's a separate, real record AR uses to track when each payment is due and to calculate late penalties. Before this fix, this record could quietly disagree with the PDC schedule for anything other than a Seafarer loan — a real, already-live bug unrelated to MPL/Salary that got fixed along the way.

Why you can't click-test this one directly

Seeing it requires taking a loan all the way through release into AR's records — not something to do casually on a shared system. I tested it directly instead: built a real Salary loan end-to-end and compared its PDC schedule against AR's own collection record.

What I found: both showed the exact same 12 dates and amounts, down to the centavo — no disagreement between what the borrower signs for and what AR actually collects against.
