# LoanStar System — Client Presentation Script

*A plain-English walkthrough for presenting the LoanStar platform to the CEO.
No technical words — this is written to be read out loud or paraphrased
naturally in the room.*

---

## How to Use This Script

- Each section has a **short purpose line** (what to tell them this part is
  for) and a **talking script** (what to actually say, in order).
- Feel free to paraphrase — the goal is that it sounds like *you* talking,
  not like you're reading a manual.
- Pause after each stage and ask, "Any questions before we move to the next
  step?" This keeps it a conversation, not a lecture.
- The whole walkthrough follows **one loan, from the moment someone applies,
  to the moment it's fully paid off** — so the CEO sees the entire journey,
  not just disconnected screens.

---

## Opening — Set the Scene

**Say:**

> "Today I want to walk you through the entire life of a loan — from the
> moment someone applies, all the way to the moment it's fully paid off.
>
> The idea behind this system is simple: a loan passes through several
> teams before it becomes money in someone's hands, and each team has one
> clear job. Instead of emails, paper folders, and spreadsheets bouncing
> around, everything lives in one system — so nothing gets lost, nothing
> gets missed, and at any moment, we know exactly where every application
> stands.
>
> I'll walk you through it step by step, in the same order a real
> application goes through. By the end, you'll have seen the whole
> pipeline — and at the very end, I'll show you the view built just for
> you: a single dashboard that shows how the whole business is doing."

---

## The Big Picture (30 seconds)

**Purpose:** Give the CEO the mental map before diving into details, so every
later screen makes sense in context.

**Say:**

> "Think of it as a relay race with eight runners. Each one has a specific
> job — checking information, making the decision, releasing the money,
> collecting payments, keeping the books — and they hand the application
> off to the next team only when their part is done properly. If something
> is wrong, it doesn't get pushed forward — it gets sent back to be fixed.
>
> Here's the order: **the borrower applies → our intake team prepares the
> file → our verification team checks that everything is true → our
> committee makes the decision → our release team hands over the money →
> and then our collection and accounting teams keep the loan running
> smoothly until it's fully paid."

---

## The Journey of a Loan

### Stage 0 — Where It All Begins: The Borrower

**Purpose:** This is the client's own portal — the entry point of everything.

**Say:**

> "Every loan starts here — with the borrower. Whether they're a seafarer
> applying before their next contract, or a small business owner applying
> for working capital, they log into their own portal and apply directly.
>
> They fill out their information, upload the documents we need — like
> valid ID, proof of income, or business documents — and submit. From that
> point on, they can log back in anytime to check their status, see exactly
> which stage their application is in, and later on, see their loan balance
> and payment history, just like online banking.
>
> They also get notified automatically at every important milestone —
> approved, needs a document, ready for release — so they're never left
> wondering what's happening."

**Why it matters:** *No more "please wait for a call" — the borrower always
knows where they stand, which means fewer phone calls asking "any update?"*

---

### Stage 1 — CSA: The Welcome & Intake Team

**Purpose:** CSA is the front desk — they make sure the application is
complete and correct before it goes any further.

**Say:**

> "Once someone applies, our CSA team — think of them as the front desk —
> picks it up. They walk the borrower through what the loan involves, do a
> short interview to confirm the basics, and go through a checklist to make
> sure every required document is there and looks right.
>
> If something is missing — say, a document is blurry or a form isn't
> complete — the system flags it immediately, so the CSA can ask the
> borrower to fix it right away, instead of the file getting stuck three
> steps later.
>
> Once everything checks out, CSA formally endorses the file to the next
> team — our verification team."

**Why it matters:** *Nothing moves forward with missing paperwork — the
checklist won't let it.*

---

### Stage 2 — CIG: The Fact-Checkers

**Purpose:** CIG verifies that everything the borrower submitted is actually
true, before we commit any money.

**Say:**

> "Next is our verification team — we call them CIG. Their whole job is to
> make sure the information is real. For a seafarer, that means confirming
> employment with the manning agency and checking references. For a small
> business, it means verifying the business itself — checking it's actually
> operating, reviewing basic financials.
>
> If anything doesn't add up, this team can send the file straight back to
> CSA with notes on what needs fixing — instead of it silently getting
> stuck or, worse, getting approved on bad information.
>
> Once the file passes verification, it moves to the team that actually
> makes the lending decision — our Committee."

**Why it matters:** *This is our safeguard against approving a loan based on
information that turns out to be false.*

---

### Stage 3 — Committee: The Decision Makers

**Purpose:** Committee is where the actual lending decision happens — approve,
decline, or negotiate.

**Say:**

> "This is the decision point. Our Committee reviews the complete,
> already-verified file — the borrower's information, the numbers, the
> computed loan terms — and votes: approve, decline, or send back for
> another look.
>
> Here's something powerful: if the committee wants to offer different
> terms than what was originally requested — say, a longer term or a
> different amount — they can send a counter-offer right in the system.
> The borrower sees it, and can accept or negotiate back, all digitally,
> with a clear record of what was agreed. No more verbal promises that are
> hard to trace later.
>
> Once both sides agree and the borrower formally accepts the terms, the
> file moves to our release team."

**Why it matters:** *Every approval, denial, and negotiation is on record —
who decided, when, and why.*

---

### Stage 4 — LRA: The Release Team

**Purpose:** LRA is where the loan becomes real — documents are signed and
the money actually moves.

**Say:**

> "This is the moment the loan becomes real. Our release team, LRA,
> prepares all the final documents, the borrower comes in-branch to sign
> everything in person — including post-dated checks for those loan types
> that use them — and the team does one last briefing to make sure the
> borrower fully understands their repayment schedule before anything is
> released.
>
> Once signing is complete, the funds are released, and the loan account
> officially goes 'live' — it now shows up as an active loan, with its own
> repayment schedule."

**Why it matters:** *The borrower signs knowing exactly what they owe and
when — no surprises down the road.*

---

### Stage 5 — Collector: Keeping the Loan on Track

**Purpose:** Collectors are the ongoing relationship — following up on
monthly payments once the loan is active.

**Say:**

> "Now the loan is active, and this is where our collection team comes in.
> Each collector is assigned a set of accounts, and their job is to follow
> up with borrowers every month, receive payments, and log them in the
> system.
>
> At the end of the day, a collector batches everything they've collected
> into a single report and submits it to our accounting team for
> reconciliation — instead of cash and receipts floating around
> unaccounted for.
>
> If an account starts falling behind, the system automatically flags it —
> so we catch a problem early, not three months later."

**Why it matters:** *Every peso collected is logged the same day, with a
paper trail back to who collected it.*

---

### Stage 6 — AR: The Accounting Safety Net

**Purpose:** AR makes sure every collection report actually matches the money
that landed in the bank — before anything gets posted to a borrower's
account.

**Say:**

> "Here's a control most lending companies don't have. Before any payment
> gets officially posted to a borrower's account, our accounting team
> checks the collector's daily report against what actually landed in the
> bank. If the numbers match, it gets posted and the borrower's balance
> updates.
>
> But if something's off — say, a collector's report doesn't match the
> actual deposit — accounting can **reject it right in the system**, with a
> reason attached. The collector gets notified, the borrower gets notified
> that their payment needs re-checking, and nothing gets posted to anyone's
> account until it's corrected. Nothing slips through by accident."

**Why it matters:** *This is our built-in checkpoint against errors — and
it's fully traceable, not a verbal "please double check" over the phone.*

---

### Stage 7 — Remedial: Helping Accounts That Fall Behind

**Purpose:** For accounts that go significantly overdue, a dedicated team
takes over to work on recovery.

**Say:**

> "Not every account stays perfectly on schedule — and for the ones that
> fall seriously behind, we don't just leave them with the regular
> collector. They get formally turned over to our Remedial team, who
> specialize in working with borrowers to get the account current again,
> or arrange a recovery plan.
>
> This means troubled accounts get focused attention, instead of being
> just one more account on a busy collector's list."

**Why it matters:** *Overdue accounts get a dedicated specialist, not just
another phone call from an already-busy collector.*

---

### Stage 8 — The Executive View: Your Dashboard

**Purpose:** This is the part built specifically for leadership — a single
place to see the health of the entire business.

**Say:**

> "And finally — this is the part I'm most excited to show you, because
> it's built specifically for someone in your seat.
>
> Instead of asking five different teams for five different reports, this
> dashboard gives you the whole business at a glance:
>
> - **How much money is moving** — how much we've released, how much we've
>   collected, and what's still outstanding.
> - **How healthy the portfolio is** — which accounts are on time, which
>   are falling behind, and how concentrated our risk is in a handful of
>   large borrowers.
> - **How fast new loans are coming in** — from application to release,
>   and where files tend to get stuck.
> - **How the team is performing** — collections per collector, how
>   quickly Committee makes decisions, and more.
>
> You can filter it by any time period — this month, this quarter, this
> year, or a custom range — export anything to a spreadsheet, or print the
> whole thing as a PDF for a board meeting.
>
> And one more thing — you'll notice a small assistant panel on this page.
> It's not active yet, but it's already built to eventually let you simply
> *ask* a question — like 'why did collections drop last month?' — and get
> an answer straight from this same data. That's coming next."

**Why it matters:** *This turns "let me get back to you with the numbers"
into "let me just show you, right now."*

---

## Why This Matters — The Bigger Picture

**Say:**

> "Stepping back — what does all of this actually give you as the owner of
> this business?
>
> First, **speed** — files don't sit in someone's inbox; they move through
> clear, tracked stages.
>
> Second, **accountability** — every action, every approval, every
> collection, every rejection is logged. If a question ever comes up about
> what happened on a specific loan, we don't have to guess — we can look it
> up.
>
> Third, **control** — the accounting checkpoint means money doesn't get
> posted to the wrong account by mistake, and risky accounts get flagged
> automatically instead of being discovered by accident.
>
> And finally, **visibility** — you, as CEO, don't have to wait for a
> report to be compiled. You open one page and see the whole business."

## Anticipated Questions (Presenter Notes)

*Keep this section for yourself — don't read it aloud unless asked.*

- **"What if a borrower disputes a rejected payment?"** — The rejection
  always comes with a written reason, and the original payment record is
  never deleted — it stays visible in the borrower's and collector's
  history, so there's always a full record to review.
- **"Can staff see things outside their own job?"** — No — each team only
  sees the workspace relevant to their role. A collector doesn't see
  committee votes, for example.
- **"How fast can we onboard a new team member?"** — Since each portal only
  shows what that role needs, training is focused on one small workspace,
  not the entire system.
